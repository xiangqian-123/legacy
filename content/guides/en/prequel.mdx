The short answer: **Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy is a prequel** — it's the prequel to A Plague Tale: Requiem, set 15 years earlier.

## The Three-Game Timeline

| Order | Game | When | Protagonist | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (earliest) | **Resonance** | 15 years before Requiem | Sophia | Prequel, origin story |
| 2 | A Plague Tale: Innocence | 1348 | Amicia & Hugo | Series debut |
| 3 | A Plague Tale: Requiem | Months after Innocence | Amicia & Hugo | Sequel, story conclusion |

## The Prequel's Unique Value

**1. The protagonist is "someone we know survives"**
Sophia is Amicia and Hugo's ally in Requiem — players know from the start she lives to the future. This is a double-edged sword for the narrative: critics call it "the prequel's inherent dilemma," i.e. how to build suspense when the ending is already known.

**2. It directly reveals the Macula's origin**
The title "Resonance" points straight at the core setup: through Minoan-era flashbacks, players learn the **true origin of the Macula** for the first time — Asterion, the original Minoan-era carrier, whose Macula never manifested as rat swarms. This is a key completion of the series' worldbuilding.

**3. The story is fairly self-contained**
Officially, you can understand this story without the first two games; but series veterans catch more easter eggs and better understand Sophia's motives in Requiem.

## The Gameplay Shift

The prequel isn't just a step back in time — it's a **reboot of the gameplay formula**:

- Innocence & Requiem: stealth-survival, rat-swarm pressure, duo interactive narrative
- Resonance: dynamic melee, environmental puzzles, action-adventure — critics frequently compare it to "Uncharted" and "Tomb Raider"

> Official note: unlike Innocence's stealth-first pacing, this game has **noticeably higher combat intensity from the very start**.
