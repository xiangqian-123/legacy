The A Plague Tale series, developed by Asobo Studio and published by Focus Entertainment, spans three games that chart its evolution from a dark-horse indie hit to a 3A action-adventure.

## Three-Game Overview

| Game | Release | Platforms | Protagonist | Gameplay tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Innocence | 2019 | PS4/Xbox One/PC | Amicia & Hugo | Stealth-survival, rat-swarm pressure |
| Requiem | 2022 | PS5/XSX/PC | Amicia & Hugo | Stealth + light combat, open-feel areas |
| **Resonance** | **2026** | **PS5/XSX/PC** | **Sophia** | **Melee action + puzzles** |

## A Plague Tale: Innocence (2019)

The series debut. In 1348 plague-ravaged France, teenage Amicia protects her sickly brother Hugo amid rat swarms and the Inquisition. Its iconic "rat sea" visual spectacle and touching sibling bond made it a breakout hit and established the series' "emotion-driven narrative" signature.

## A Plague Tale: Requiem (2022)

The sequel pushes the story to its peak: Hugo's Macula bloodline erupts, and the siblings flee to Provence. It adds more combat atop the stealth, dramatically upgrades the visuals, and is called "a modern masterpiece" by many — with a Metascore above the first game. Sophia appears here as an ally.

## Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy (2026)

The series hands the protagonist role to someone outside Amicia's family for the first time — prequel protagonist Sophia. Gameplay turns fully toward action-adventure:

- Dynamic melee (kicks, parries, Focus Attacks) replaces stealth pressure
- Light-reflection puzzles become the core loop
- Dual timeline: medieval plunderer ↔ Minoan-era hero Theseus
- Directly reveals the Macula's origin

## Two Lines of Evolution

**Narrative line**: the Amicia & Hugo sibling story (Innocence + Requiem) → Sophia's origin story (Resonance). The core theme remains "protecting your loved ones through plague and darkness."

**Gameplay line**: stealth-survival (Innocence) → stealth-first with combat (Requiem) → action-adventure (Resonance). This shift is the game's biggest point of debate — veterans miss the stealth pressure, newcomers welcome the snappier combat.

> Community consensus: all three games "tell good stories, but play very differently." Chapter 6 What Lies Beneath is the most "true to the series' roots" chapter, where the rat-swarm dread briefly returns.
