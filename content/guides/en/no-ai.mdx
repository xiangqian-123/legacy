In 2026, as generative AI sweeps the games industry, Asobo Studio made a rare public statement for Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy: **it does not use generative AI in development**. This choice became one of the game's most talked-about behind-the-scenes stories.

## Core Facts

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Engine | **Zouna** (Asobo's in-house game engine) |
| Team size | About **70 people** (lean by 3A standards) |
| Producer | **Eric Chort** |
| AI stance | Explicitly no generative AI, which it sees as threatening "creativity and authenticity" |

## What the Producer Said

Producer Eric Chort on generative AI:

- Calls using generative AI a "**choice**" in game development
- Doesn't see AI as the "**solution**" for this ~70-person team to "compete with big-studio 3A teams"

In plain terms: Asobo's attitude is "we're small, but we don't lean on AI to make up for it" — sticking with traditional craft for art, narrative and hand-tuned puzzles. That's a footnote to why the game's "visuals and art" are doubly praised by critics and players.

## Why This Resonated

1. **Against the tide**: 2026 is the year generative AI entered game development en masse — openly saying "no AI" is itself news
2. **It matches the reception**: the game's strengths (art, puzzles, atmosphere) are exactly the parts where human craft is most visible
3. **Industry discussion value**: "should small teams use AI to chase big studios" is a core industry debate right now, and Asobo offers a counter-example
